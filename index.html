The Problem

Africa doesn't have
an agriculture problem.
It has a visibility problem.

01

Farmers are invisible

600 million Africans work in agriculture. The people growing the continent's food have no media profile, no digital presence, and no clear path to attracting the capital their work deserves.

02

Capital is disconnected

Impact investors, agri-fintechs, input suppliers, and development finance institutions have no reliable channel to discover and engage with farmers at scale. The stories that could unlock billions go untold.

03

Media is failing the sector

The few agriculture outlets in Africa are government bulletins or narrow trade press. None are building farmer-centric, editorially excellent content for the modern internet. The gap is enormous and growing.

Why Now

Six forces are converging.
The window is open today.

🌍

Africa's Digital Leap

Smartphone penetration is accelerating across sub-Saharan Africa. Rural farmers are coming online for the first time — and there is no platform built for them.

🤖

AI Makes Editorial Cheap

The cost of producing, curating, and distributing editorial content has collapsed. A lean team can now do what required a newsroom of thirty. The content moat is buildable today.

💸

Capital Chasing Agriculture

DFIs, climate funds, and agri-VCs are deploying billions into African agriculture. That capital has no media layer to discover, validate, or amplify the farmers it's trying to reach.

🌾

Young Africans Entering Farming

A new generation — the most connected in African history — is entering agriculture. They will demand digital identity, media presence, and market connections that their parents never had.

📢

Global Food Security Moment

Food security is a top-of-agenda conversation globally. Africa is central to that story. Investors, governments, and consumers are primed to engage with African farming content like never before.

🎥

Creator Economy Ignoring Agriculture

The creator economy is generating billions — but agriculture has no dedicated home. Farmers documenting their work on TikTok and Instagram have nowhere to convert content into professional identity or income.
